The Invisible List

Lauren Fine, MD Ten minutes Fill it in here or print it

In medicine we keep a problem list. Every active issue, written down, so nothing lives only in one person's head. When a patient changes teams, the list goes with them.

Most households do not have one. The list exists. It just lives in one or two heads. That is why it feels heavy even on a quiet day.

This worksheet gets it out of your head and onto paper. Then you decide who actually owns each item. You can type right on this page. Your answers stay in your own browser and are never sent anywhere.

Step one

Brain dump

Write down everything you are tracking. Big and small. One item per line. Use the prompts to jog your memory.

Step two

Mark who owns it

Owning means three things: noticing it needs doing, planning it, and doing it. If you do the noticing and someone else does the task, you still own most of it. This idea comes from Eve Rodsky's Fair Play.

M Me, start to finish
P My partner, start to finish
S Shared, and we both know who does which part
K The kids could own this
O Outsource or automate it
D Drop it. Nobody will miss it.

Step three

Pick two

One item to hand off completely. One item to drop. That is all.

Step four

Try it for two weeks

When you hand something off, hand off all three parts. A way to say it:

"Can you own dinners this week from start to finish? Remembering it, planning it, and doing it. I will not check on it."

Then do not check on it. Two weeks, no micromanaging, no hovering or nagging. Notice what changes.
