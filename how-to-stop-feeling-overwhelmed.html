How to Stop Feeling Overwhelmed When Everything Feels Urgent

one next step, then the next

Lauren Fine, MD November 1, 2026 10 min read

The inbox is full. The kids need forms signed. A deadline moved up. Someone you love needs a call back. You sit down to start and nothing happens. Your brain feels like a browser with forty tabs open, and every one of them is playing sound.

That is overwhelm. It is common, it is uncomfortable, and it responds to action more than to willpower. This guide gives you a plan for the next ten minutes, a plan for this week, and clear signs that it is time to get more help.

A quick note before we start. I am a physician, but this page is coaching, not medical advice. If what you feel is heavier than overwhelm, skip to when to get medical or mental health help.

Why do I feel so overwhelmed?

Overwhelm shows up when demands outrun your capacity to meet them. Too many tasks. Too many roles. Too little time, sleep or support. Often all of it at once.

You are not imagining the load. In Gallup's State of the Global Workplace: 2026 Report, 40% of employees worldwide said they experienced stress a lot of the previous day. In the United States, the figure was 50%. Half the workforce, on any given day.

Parents carry even more. In the American Psychological Association's Stress in America 2023 survey, 48% of parents of children under 18 said most days their stress is completely overwhelming. Among other adults, it was 26%. Forty-one percent of parents said most days they are too stressed to function.

A few patterns make overwhelm worse for high achievers:

  • Everything lives in your head. When your only list is your memory, your brain keeps rehearsing it all day.
  • Everything feels equally urgent. Without a sort, the permission slip and the grant deadline get the same alarm.
  • You said yes too many times. Each yes was reasonable. Together they built a week that was never possible.
  • No one else is holding the plan. The invisible work of remembering, planning and checking falls on one person. I cover that in how to reduce mental load.

Overwhelmed vs. stressed: what is the difference?

People use the words interchangeably, and they overlap. A useful working difference: stress is pressure you can still push against. You feel tense, but you can act. Overwhelm is the point where the pressure feels bigger than you. You stop prioritizing. You stall, scroll or snap.

Stress often has a clear source and an end date. A presentation on Friday. A hard conversation on Monday. Overwhelm tends to come from many sources piling up at once, with no clear finish line. That is why the fix for overwhelm usually starts with getting everything out of your head and sorting it, not with pushing harder.

When overwhelmed, what do I do in the next 10 minutes?

You do not need to solve your whole life right now. You need to get from frozen to moving. Set a timer and try this sequence.

  1. Minute 1: Stop and land. Put both feet on the floor. Take a slow breath in and a longer breath out. Do it three times. You are telling yourself that this moment is manageable.
  2. Minutes 2 to 5: Empty your head. Grab paper or a notes app. Write every task, worry and loose end you can think of. Do not sort. Do not judge. Just get it out.
  3. Minute 6: Circle what is due today. Truly due, with a real consequence if it slips. Usually it is one to three things, not twenty.
  4. Minute 7: Pick one. Choose the circled item that would bring the most relief once it is done.
  5. Minutes 8 to 10: Do the first tiny piece. Open the document. Write the first line of the email. Make the one phone call. Or send one message that buys you time: "I need until Thursday for this."

That is it. Ten minutes will not empty the list. It will show you that the list is finite, and that you can still move. Momentum does more for overwhelm than any pep talk.

How do I stop feeling overwhelmed this week?

The ten-minute reset gets you through today. This plan changes the shape of the week. Give it about an hour, once.

1. Do a full brain dump

Write down everything on your plate. Work projects, household tasks, appointments, favors you promised, things you keep meaning to do. Include the small ones. The small ones are often the loudest.

2. Sort it

Go through the list and mark each item with one letter:

  • M for must. It has a real deadline or a real consequence.
  • S for should. It matters, but the timing is flexible.
  • C for could. Nice to do. No one will notice if it waits.

3. Drop, delegate or defer

Now look at everything that is not an M. For each item, ask three questions:

  • Can I drop it? Some tasks exist only because no one ever questioned them.
  • Can I delegate it? To a partner, a colleague, a kid old enough to help, or a service you can pay for.
  • Can I defer it? Give it a real date next month and take it off this week.

Most people find they can clear a third of the list this way. If you want to see where your hours are going first, the free Find Your Hour time audit takes ten minutes.

4. Set one boundary

Overwhelm comes back if new demands keep landing at the same rate. Pick one boundary that protects your week. No email after 8 p.m. One meeting-free morning. A standing answer for new requests: "Let me check my calendar and get back to you." Just one. If you need words, these scripts for saying no help, and how to set boundaries walks through the harder cases.

5. Try it for two weeks

Repeat the brain dump and sort every Sunday or Monday. Keep the one boundary. At the end of two weeks, notice what changed. Did the list get shorter? Did you sleep better? Did anything fall apart when you let it go? Usually less than you feared.

What should I do when I'm overwhelmed at work?

Work overwhelm has its own flavor. Priorities shift without warning. Meetings eat the time you planned for real work. Everyone has a reason their request is urgent. Here is how to not get overwhelmed at work as often:

  • Ask your manager to rank it. "I have these five projects. Which two matter most this month?" You are not admitting failure. You are asking for the information you need to do the job well.
  • Block time for deep work. Put two focused blocks on your calendar each week and treat them like meetings.
  • Batch the small stuff. Check email and messages at set times, not every time a notification lights up.
  • Close the day on purpose. Spend five minutes before you leave writing tomorrow's first task. You stop carrying it home in your head.
  • Watch your yes reflex. If you say yes before you have checked your capacity, read how to stop people pleasing.

Should I quit if I'm overwhelmed at work?

Maybe. Maybe not yet. Overwhelmed at work and ready to quit is a common place to land, and the answer depends on the source.

If the overwhelm comes from a fixable load, like too many projects, unclear priorities or a missing boundary, try the plan above first. Give it two weeks. Sometimes the job is fine and the week was broken.

If the overwhelm comes from the job itself, the picture changes. A role that conflicts with your values. A culture that punishes limits. Work that drains you even on light weeks. Then the question is real, and it deserves a careful answer. Should I quit my job? walks through how to decide, and how to be happy at work covers what to change if you stay.

What are the signs of an overwhelmed mom?

Mothers often notice overwhelm last, because they are busy noticing everyone else. Common signs:

  • You snap at the kids over small things, then feel guilty all evening.
  • You lie awake running tomorrow's list.
  • You forget things you would normally never forget.
  • You feel touched out, talked out and done by 7 p.m.
  • You cannot remember the last time you did something just for you.

Tips for overwhelmed moms start with the same brain dump and sort. Then add a few home-specific moves:

  • Share the list, not just the tasks. Asking for help with one chore still leaves you as the manager. Hand over a whole area, like school forms or meal planning, from start to finish.
  • Lower one standard. Frozen dinner on Wednesdays. Laundry folded once a week. Pick a place to be good enough.
  • Protect one hour. Even one hour a week that belongs to you changes the tone of the week. See how to make time for yourself.

If the bigger issue is that your work and home lives are both full, work-life balance looks at why a vacation alone will not fix it.

Why do I shut down when I'm overwhelmed?

Many people describe the same thing. Too much at once, and they freeze. They stare at the screen. They scroll. They nap. They avoid the very thing they need to do.

Shutting down does not mean you are lazy. It is a common response when every option feels too big to start. The way out is to make the next step smaller than the resistance. Not "finish the report." Open the file. Not "clean the house." Clear one counter.

Be kind to yourself in the freeze. Criticism adds weight. A tiny action removes some. Use the ten-minute sequence above, and if you only get as far as minute one, that still counts.

How long does feeling overwhelmed last?

It depends on the cause. Overwhelm tied to a specific crunch, like a move, a deadline or a new baby, often eases once that period passes or once you change how you handle the load. Overwhelm from a life that is built too full tends to stay until something in the structure changes.

That is why the two-week test matters. If you clear your list, set a boundary and get some rest, and you still feel overwhelmed most days, pay attention. That is useful information, and it may mean you need more support than a plan.

When should I get medical or mental health help?

Overwhelm is a normal human experience. Sometimes it overlaps with, or masks, something that needs medical or mental health care. Coaching is not a substitute for that care. Talk to your doctor or a licensed mental health professional if:

  • The feeling lasts for weeks and does not ease when your load gets lighter.
  • You cannot function at work, at home or in your relationships.
  • You have changes in sleep, appetite or energy that do not go away.
  • You feel hopeless, numb or panicky most days.
  • You are using alcohol or other substances to get through the day.

If you are having thoughts of harming yourself, get help now. In the United States, call or text 988 to reach the 988 Suicide and Crisis Lifeline, available 24 hours a day. If you are in immediate danger, call 911 or go to the nearest emergency room.

Not sure whether you need a coach or a therapist? Read life coach vs. therapist: which do you need? Many people benefit from both at different times.

Make room

Overwhelm tells you that everything matters equally and that it all has to happen now. Neither is true. Get it out of your head. Sort it. Drop, delegate or defer what you can. Set one boundary. Try it for two weeks and notice what changes.

Frequently asked questions

How do I stop feeling overwhelmed fast?

Take three slow breaths, then write down everything in your head. Circle the one to three things truly due today, pick one, and do the smallest first piece. Ten minutes of action usually breaks the freeze.

What is the difference between overwhelmed and stressed?

Stress is pressure you can still push against, often with a clear source and end date. Overwhelm is when the pressure feels bigger than your capacity, usually from many demands piling up at once, and you stall or shut down.

Why do I shut down when I'm overwhelmed?

Shutting down is a common response when every option feels too big to start. Make the next step smaller than the resistance, like opening the file instead of finishing the report.

How long does feeling overwhelmed last?

Overwhelm tied to a specific crunch often eases when that period passes or the load changes. If it lasts for weeks after you lighten your load, talk to your doctor or a mental health professional.

Should I quit my job if I feel overwhelmed at work?

Not before you know the source. Try ranking priorities with your manager, setting one boundary and cutting what you can for two weeks. If the job itself is the problem, the question of leaving deserves a careful decision.

When is feeling overwhelmed a sign I need professional help?

Get help if it lasts for weeks, keeps you from functioning, or comes with hopelessness or changes in sleep and appetite. If you have thoughts of harming yourself, call or text 988 in the US to reach the 988 Suicide and Crisis Lifeline.
