Permission

I Didn't Ask
Anyone What
They Thought

on trusting yourself before the proof arrives

Lauren Fine, MD August 27, 2026 4 min read

A few weeks ago I started building something that has nothing to do with medicine, coaching, or anything else on my resume: a wardrobe edit. Pieces I actually wear, styled for the way a real professional day actually moves.

I didn't run it by anyone first.

Not because I don't value other people's opinions. I do, deeply. But I've learned something over the years that I keep having to relearn: if you wait for consensus before you start, you'll wait forever. Someone will always have a reason it's not the right time, not the right fit, not what you're "known for."

They're not wrong to wonder. I wondered too. A physician who also talks about blazers and travel packing lists is, on paper, an odd combination. It doesn't fit neatly into a bio. It's not what I trained for.

But it's honest. I live the exact problem this edit solves: not enough time, too many hats, and a closet that was never built for the actual shape of my week. If that's true for me, it's true for a lot of people who look a lot like me. That was reason enough to start.

Permission Is the Whole Point

This is exactly the thing I write about under Permission to Change: you don't need anyone's approval to become the next version of yourself. Not your colleagues'. Not your followers'. Not mine, if you're the one standing at your own crossroads right now.

Waiting for permission is just fear wearing a patient face.

I've watched this pattern in coaching clients for years before I recognized it fully in myself. The pattern is always the same: a good idea, a real pull toward it, and then a slow, well-reasoned campaign to talk themselves out of it by imagining what everyone else would say.

The people who eventually move aren't the ones who get everyone on board first. They're the ones who decide it's worth doing even if no one claps yet.

What This Actually Looks Like

Practically, it looked like this: I had an idea, I built the first version of it, and I put it out into the world before it was polished, before I had a plan for every objection, before I could tell you exactly where it was going.

It still doesn't have all its pieces. There's more to build. But it exists now, which it wouldn't if I'd waited for it to feel fully safe first.

You don't need permission. You need to start.

If there's something you've been sitting on, waiting for the right moment or the right nod from someone else, consider this your reminder that the moment doesn't announce itself. You just have to decide.

If you want to see where this particular leap of faith landed, here's the wardrobe edit. And if you're sitting on your own version of this, wondering whether it's allowed, let's talk it through.
