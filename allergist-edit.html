Comfort.

I am a board certified allergist and immunologist, and the question I get most often has nothing to do with medication. It is some version of: what is actually safe to put on my skin, sleep in, and wash with?

This is that answer, in the order I would actually change things, plus the pieces I reach for once I'm dressed and out the door. For the office, I keep a separate professional wardrobe edit.

Start here

Three words on the label. Only one is checkable.

Hypoallergenic

Not regulated

There is no standard behind it and no test a product has to pass. Any company can print it on anything. It is the word people trust most and the one that tells you least.

Unscented

Often misleading

A product can be unscented and still contain fragrance, added specifically to mask the smell of the other ingredients. If you react to fragrance, this word does not protect you.

Fragrance free

Checkable

It means none was added, and you can confirm it on the ingredient list. Together with "allergy tested," that is what I look for.

A note on how to use this. Nothing here treats a condition. If your skin is genuinely inflamed, or you have eczema or hives that are not controlled, that is a conversation with your own physician, not a shopping list.

What this list does is reduce what your skin has to deal with in the first place. Fewer irritants, less sun, less friction, fewer dust mites. That is unglamorous and it is most of the work.

Change one thing at a time. If everything changes at once and your skin improves, you have learned nothing about which change mattered.

Step one

Cover comes before cream

Sunscreen is the last line of defense, not the first. Fabric does not wear off at hour two, does not need reapplying, and does not depend on you using the right amount. Put cloth between you and the sun first, then use SPF for what the cloth does not reach.
