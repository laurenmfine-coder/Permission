How to keep work and personal life separate

let work stay at work.

Lauren Fine, MD November 18, 2026 6 min read

You close the laptop, but your head stays at work. You are at dinner replaying a meeting. You are at your kid's game checking email under the bleachers. Your job ends at 6 on paper. In real life, it doesn't end at all.

Keeping work and personal life separate is not about caring less about your job. It is about building enough of a wall that your work stays at work and your life gets the rest of you. Here is how to do it, whether you go to an office or work from home.

Why work follows you home

  • Your phone is your office. Work email and chat live on the same device as your family photos.
  • There is no commute. Working from home removed the drive that used to switch your brain off.
  • Open loops. Unfinished tasks keep running in the background until you write them down.
  • Identity. When work is who you are, it is hard to stop being it at 6 p.m.

Eight ways to keep work and personal life separate

1. Set a shutdown ritual

End every workday the same way. Write tomorrow's top three tasks, close every tab, and say out loud that you are done. The ritual tells your brain the day is over.

2. Take work off your phone, or hide it

Move work apps off your home screen, turn off notifications after hours, or use a focus mode that silences them. If your company allows it, a separate work phone that stays in a drawer is even better.

3. Make a fake commute

If you work from home, walk around the block before and after work. Ten minutes outside gives you the transition a drive used to.

4. Keep a work spot

Work in one place, and don't work in the places you rest. Not the bed. Not the couch you watch movies on. When the laptop closes, leave the spot.

5. Write the open loops down

The meeting you keep replaying needs a place to go. Write down what is unfinished and when you will deal with it. Then it can stop running in your head.

6. Tell people your hours

Say when you are reachable and how to reach you for real emergencies. Most people will respect a limit they know about. Scripts are in setting boundaries at work.

7. Protect one thing outside work

A class, a standing dinner, a Saturday run. One commitment that work does not get to cancel makes everything else easier to protect.

8. Have a life work cannot reach

Friends who do not work with you. A hobby that has nothing to do with your job. The more of you that lives outside work, the less power work has over the rest.

How to decenter work from your life

For many high achievers, the deeper problem is not hours. It is that work became the center everything else orbits. Decentering work means letting other things matter as much: your health, your people, the parts of you that have nothing to do with a title.

Start by noticing what you say when someone asks about you. If the only answer is your job, there is room to grow other answers. It does not happen in a weekend. It happens one protected evening at a time.

When you can't separate them

Some jobs blur the line by design: running your own business, on-call work, caring for clients who need you at odd hours. In those cases, aim for control rather than separation. Decide when work gets to come in, and make those windows as predictable as you can. If the job never lets you choose, that is worth a harder look. See should I quit my job.

Frequently asked questions

How do I keep work and personal life separate?

Use a shutdown ritual at the end of the day, take work off your phone or silence it after hours, keep a dedicated work spot, write down open loops, and tell people when you are reachable.

How do I separate work and home when working from home?

Create a fake commute with a short walk, work in one spot that is not where you rest, close the laptop at a set time, and keep work notifications off in the evening.

How do I stop thinking about work after hours?

Write down everything unfinished and when you will deal with it, then do something that fully absorbs you. Open loops keep running until they have somewhere to go.

What does it mean to decenter work?

It means letting your health, relationships and interests matter as much as your job, so work is one part of your life rather than the center everything else orbits.

Should I have a separate work phone?

If your employer allows it, a separate work phone that stays in a drawer after hours is one of the simplest ways to keep work from following you home.
