How to Set Boundaries When You’re the One Everyone Relies On

every yes is a no to something else

Lauren Fine, MD October 9, 2026 6 min read

You are the reliable one. The person who covers the shift, answers the late email, hosts the holiday, and says "no problem" before the question is finished. People count on you. That is a good thing. Until it costs you your evenings, your health, and the people you love most.

Every yes is a no to something else. Boundaries are how you decide what gets the yes. This guide shows you how to set boundaries at work and with family, with examples and scripts you can use this week.

What a boundary actually is

A boundary is a decision about your own behavior. It is not a rule for someone else.

"Please stop emailing me at night" is a request. The other person can ignore it. "I don't answer email after 7 p.m." is a boundary. It works no matter what anyone else does.

This is good news. You do not need anyone's permission or cooperation to set a boundary. You only need to decide, and then follow through.

Why am I so bad at boundaries?

You are not bad at boundaries. You are very good at something else. Most high achievers were rewarded for years for saying yes. Yes got you the grades, the job, the promotion, the reputation. Your brain learned that yes is safe.

There is a second reason. The yes is fast and the cost is slow. You feel relief the moment you agree. You feel the cost two weeks later, at 10 p.m., doing the thing you never wanted to do.

The third reason is the story. "If I say no, they'll think less of me." "If I don't do it, no one will." These feel like facts. Usually they are habits. I use a simple tool called Fact vs. Story: write down what is actually true, then what you are telling yourself about it. The gap between the two is where your boundary lives.

When I began working in academic medicine I felt that I had not earned the right to set boundaries by saying no. How could I do that when I was the most junior person there? And each subsequent opportunity to say no felt the same, like I was still too green, too inexperienced, and had not earned the right to. It really took someone who was a mentor to me to tell me it was ok to set boundaries, but this person also taught me HOW, and that is so important. Because setting boundaries is not about becoming a “no” person. It is about saying yes to what you need to say yes to. And as in any other negotiation, any opportunity to say yes to something the other person wants rather than just saying no to what you don’t want can help build the strength of your relationship.

How to set boundaries in five steps

1. Find the leak

You cannot protect time you cannot see. For one week, write down every yes. Next to each one, write what it cost: an hour, a dinner, a workout, sleep. The Yes Invoice is a free worksheet that does exactly this.

2. Decide before you are asked

Boundaries fail in the moment because you are deciding under pressure. Decide now. Write one rule you will follow. "I leave by 5:30 on Tuesdays." "I don't take new committee work this year." A rule made in advance is much easier to keep.

3. Buy time

I want you to stop answering on the spot. Use this sentence: "Let me check my calendar and get back to you tomorrow." That is the 24-Hour Rule. Most of the yeses you regret were given in under ten seconds.

4. Say it once, and keep it short

Long explanations invite negotiation. One sentence is enough. "I can't take that on right now." "That doesn't work for me." "No is a complete sentence, but a script helps."

5. Hold it on the second ask

The first no is easy. The second ask is where most boundaries fall apart. Someone pushes back, adds guilt, or says "just this once." Repeat yourself calmly. "I understand. My answer is still no." The Second Ask has word-for-word replies for guilt, urgency, and the repeat ask.

The 3 C's of boundary setting

You will see different versions of the 3 C's online. The version I teach is Clear, Calm, and Consistent.

  • Clear. Say exactly what you will and will not do. "I'm not available on weekends" beats "I'll try to keep weekends lighter."
  • Calm. No apology tour. No defending. A boundary delivered calmly is heard as a fact.
  • Consistent. A boundary you keep only sometimes teaches people to ask twice. Hold it every time for two weeks, and the asking slows down.

Healthy boundary examples

Boundaries at work

  • "I don't check messages after 7 p.m. If it's urgent, call me."
  • "I can take this on if we move the deadline on the other project. Which one matters more?"
  • "I'm at capacity this month. Can we revisit in November?"
  • "I need meeting agendas a day ahead, or I'll skip the meeting and read the notes."
  • "I'm not the right person for this, but here is who might be."

Boundaries with family

  • "We're hosting Thanksgiving this year, but not the whole weekend."
  • "I love you, and I'm not going to discuss my career choices at dinner."
  • "I can't help with that this week. I can help next Saturday."
  • "Sunday mornings are for our family. I'll call you in the afternoon."
  • "If the conversation turns into yelling, I'm going to leave and we can try again later."

Boundaries with yourself

  • "I don't open my laptop in bed."
  • "I don't say yes to anything new without naming what it replaces."
  • "When it's good enough, I call it done."

How to set boundaries at work without hurting your career

Most people fear that a boundary will make them look less committed. In practice, a clear boundary makes you look like someone who knows their priorities. The trick is to frame the no around the work, not around you.

Instead of "I'm too busy," try "If I take this on, the Henderson report slips. Which do you want first?" You are not refusing. You are making the trade visible so the person who asked can make it with you.

How to set boundaries with family

Family boundaries are harder because the history is longer and the guilt is louder. Start small. Pick one recurring situation that drains you, and decide one thing you will do differently. Tell the person once, kindly, before the situation comes up. Then follow through.

If a family relationship feels unsafe, not just frustrating, start with a licensed therapist. A coach is the right partner for building skills and making decisions. A therapist is the right partner for safety and for healing old wounds. Not sure which you need? The free Coach or Therapist check takes two minutes.

What unhealthy boundaries look like

  • You say yes before the person finishes asking.
  • You feel resentment more often than you feel tired.
  • You can list every commitment on your calendar but can't remember the last thing you did just for you.
  • You over-explain every no, then apologize for it.
  • You do other people's work because it is faster than asking them to do it.
  • Your family gets the leftover version of you.

Make room

You will never find time. You make room. Boundaries are how you make it. Pick one boundary from this page. Try it for two weeks. Then notice what you got back.

Frequently asked questions

What are 5 examples of healthy boundaries?

Not answering work messages after a set time. Asking for a day before you commit. Leaving a conversation that turns into yelling. Protecting one family block each week. Saying no to new commitments without naming what they replace.

What are the three main types of boundaries?

Lists vary, but most group boundaries into physical (your body and space), emotional (what you take on from others), and time (how your hours are spent). For high achievers, time boundaries are usually the place to start.

What is the golden rule of boundaries?

You cannot control what other people do. You can control what you do next. Every boundary that works is built on that.

Is setting boundaries selfish?

No. A boundary protects your ability to show up well for the people and work that matter most. Running on empty helps no one.

Why do people get upset when I set a boundary?

Because the old arrangement worked for them. Some pushback is normal. Stay calm, repeat yourself once, and give it two weeks. Most people adjust faster than you expect.
