Lauren Fine's Edit

What's actually in your bed.

Dust mites don't live on you, they live in your mattress, your pillows, and your sheets, feeding on the skin cells you shed every night. Pet dander is smaller still and clings to fabric the same way. Neither one cares how expensive your bedding is. What they care about is whether it ever sees hot water.

This is the edit I actually use to keep both under control, starting with what's on the bed and working out from there.

Wash hot, or use the dryer hot. Studies presented at the American Thoracic Society found that laundry washed at 140°F (60°C) killed 100 percent of dust mites, compared with only about 6.5 percent at a warm 104°F (40°C) wash. An older study in the Journal of Allergy and Clinical Immunology found the same thing: mites died reliably at water temperatures of 55°C (131°F) or higher. If your washer can't hit that temperature, or the fabric can't take it, the dryer is the fix, at least 15 minutes on a cycle above 130°F kills mites that survived the wash.

This is why washability matters more than almost anything else you'll read about "hypoallergenic" bedding. A sheet that can't tolerate hot water and a hot dryer is a sheet that can't actually get dust mites out of your bed, no matter what the label says. Pet dander responds to the same heat, which is why one laundry routine handles both.

Step three

Pet dander needs to leave the air, not just the bed

Dander particles are small enough to stay airborne for hours, so washing the bedding handles what's landed, an air purifier handles what hasn't yet. Look for the Asthma & Allergy Friendly® Certification Mark specifically, it's the one independent, third-party program (run by the Asthma and Allergy Foundation of America with Allergy Standards Limited) that actually tests whether a purifier removes bio-allergens like pet dander rather than just moving them around the room.

Sources

  • Park, J-W., et al. "Optimal Conditions for Mechanical Laundry in the Removal of House Dust Mites, Dog and Pollen Allergens." Presented at the American Thoracic Society 2007 International Conference. Hot water (140°F/60°C or higher) killed 100 percent of dust mites; warm water (104°F/40°C) killed about 6.5 percent.
  • McDonald, L.G. and Tovey, E. "The role of water temperature and laundry procedures in reducing house dust mite populations and allergen content of bedding." Journal of Allergy and Clinical Immunology, October 1992. All mites were killed at water temperatures of 55°C (131°F) or greater.
  • Asthma and Allergy Foundation of America and Allergy Standards Limited. Asthma & Allergy Friendly® Certification Program. Independent testing standard for air purifiers, vacuums, bedding, and other household products.
