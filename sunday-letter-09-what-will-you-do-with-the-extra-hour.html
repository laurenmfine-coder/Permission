What Will You Do With
the Extra Hour?

Lauren Fine, MD November 1, 2026 5 min read

Halloween at our house has many traditions. Some of my favorites are apple cider and donuts (some of the grown-ups add bourbon to the cider), hot chocolate with brown sugar whipped cream, and pizza.

One tradition that has saved me countless hours at the dentist (I'm sure…) is the Halloween Witch. This is something my mom learned from one of her girlfriends when we were kids and ever since then, any child in our family has followed it. The legend says that if you leave most of your candy (except for a handful of your favorites) out for the Halloween Witch, she will leave you money in exchange. The amount she leaves has had to increase over the years. I used to get away with $1 but I think that given the going rate of Roblox Robux I'm going to have to convince the witch to leave $10-20 each.

The clocks went back at 2am this morning. You got an hour back today. What do you plan to do with it?

When the American Academy of Sleep Medicine asked people what they would do with the extra hour, 41 % said sleep, 13% said friends and family, and 5% said work.

Most of us will not notice where it went. So this week is not a story. It is a tool. Ten minutes, a pen and this email.

What to do with it

Three small things this week.

Keep your usual bedtime and wake time, by the new clock, all week. That is how the hour actually turns into sleep.

Do the Find Your Hour worksheet below before Wednesday.

Tell one person which hour you claimed. It is harder to give away once someone knows.

The coach's view

Most of us are not short on discipline. We are short on time that has not already been promised to someone else.

One extra hour a year will not fix that. One protected hour every week can.

This week's question: What would you do with one hour a week that no one else could claim?

Find your hour

A ten-minute worksheet

Grab a pen. Use the back of an envelope, or hit reply and type your answers to me. It is adapted from the time audit Harvard Business School professor Ashley Whillans uses in her research. More on her below.

Step one. Map a normal day. Pick a regular Tuesday. Write down what fills it. Include the scrolling.

Morning: ______________________________
Afternoon: ____________________________
Evening: ______________________________

Step two. Mark each one. M for meaningful. P for pleasant. D for draining. Some get two letters. Caring for someone you love can be M and D at the same time.

Step three. Find the leaks. Circle anything marked only D. For each one, ask three questions. Can I stop doing it? Can I hand it to someone else? Can I pay to have it done?

Step four. Claim one hour. Pick one hour this week that you will not give away. Put it on your calendar now, like any other appointment.

My hour: ____________ day, ____________ time.

Step five. Decide what it is for. Sleep, a walk, a friend, a book or nothing at all. It only has to be yours.
